it's hard being holistic (pt. 1)


I’m gonna assume a certain level of shared assumptions about the world with you, dear reader. Broadly, we know that this shit is Not On. Capitalism, fascism, racism, inequality, targeted violence, suppression, these are all horrible things. We can agree on that. I’ll go a step further and say, axiomatically, that we believe that the goal of being alive is to be happy, healthy, and self-actualised. To be loved, fed, sheltered, and enabled to pursue something that gives us a sense of purpose, whatever that is. This is, sobering as it is, not a universal belief - and to make things worse, we don’t even necessarily operate from this kind of place, where beliefs are second-order to a core truth about what matters in the world. We’re full of contradiction. Flickers of it permeate the corners of our vision every day, when we give in to frustration and rage and isolation and decide that this guy, THIS GUY, is totally ok to bully. They’ve revealed some characteristic that brushes up against our line of acceptable personhood in an uncomfy way (which, happens to be about 20% weirder than how weird we are at any given time, funny how that works) and it’s time to go to town. I’m not feigning sympathy for the chuds in the world getting their comeuppance, even if I do feel “too woke” about it. I’m just mad about the shit we do to people like Patty Taxxon. Look, the tranny has transgressive sexuality! Eeeew! The disgust response wins again. I still meet people who make jokes about Christine Chandler? A literal real-life truman show of a person? Can you imagine not only your entire life being documented against your will, but constant attempts to provoke you into making that slow-form snuff film more “interesting”? Why are we trying to socially murder anyone who’s a little more autistic than us? The disgust response wins again. I’ve gotten away from myself again, but I’m approaching my point. I’m frustrated with reduction. I’m frustrated with being reduced. I’m frustrated with seeing others being reduced.

It’s hard being holistic.

Somewhere in the past year and a half, I started describing myself as a “walking twitter bio”. It’s a self deprecating joke, one that reveals some self loathing on my part. I am a disabled, polyamorous, bisexual, plural, therian transgender woman. You see what I mean? There is probably something in that list that you don’t even think exists. These are all non-normative, core aspects of my identity, including things that I chose and things that I didn’t. I’ll leave you to argue over which is which. They are, however, ALL things that inform every interaction I have with both people and systems in my daily life. Everything I do involves some amount of running down a list of questions in my mind:

Is it possible to openly engage with and represent this part of myself right now?
Is it safe to do so?
Is it possible and safe to ask someone else to engage with this part of myself right now?
Do I have the energy to explain who and what I am right now? To get people “up to speed” on my deal?
Will it be rewarding to do so? Will it give me access to better forms of understanding, or will I need to constantly haggle for recognition?
Could I ask for genuine consideration, even concessions?
Will I be able to assume those concessions are met, or do I need to constantly re-advocate?

It’s exhausting being complicated. It feels like I’m always balancing a hundred or so different life sim-style meters that are filling or draining at any given time. You have to become an expert at deciding, often upfront, what level of mazlow’s pyramid you’re gunning for in this particular moment, this particular conversation, and so on. Let me get specific.

I am disabled. Worse, I have invisible disabilities. That means that you can’t tell at a glance that I might be struggling in the way that you would if I had, say, a wheelchair. It introduces a lot of difficulties in even getting people to recognise your disability, and articulate it to them in a way that translates to genuine empathy and understanding, as opposed to “I understand, BUT (able bodied expectation)”. I know for a fact that I have fibromyalgia, a famously noodly chronic pain condition with a lot of potential symptoms under its big tent. In daily life terms, it results in me having a lot of chronic pain all over my body, and a much lower physical resilience than I used to have. I can’t, for example, work a full-time job doing just about anything, but especially fine motor movements like using a mouse + keyboard. I have to invest in an ergonomic home office setup, be very careful when working away from my home office, and sometimes I just have a flareup that takes me out completely for hours, days, weeks. I’m dependent on medication to keep me functional, and the amount of medication I need has increased over time. I need to smoke weed most evenings to deal with it lately. The pain increases with stress, so oftentimes I have the worst flareups when I most need to be productive in spite of it. I sometimes have difficulty sleeping, and my body instinctively makes me fairly numb, which gets in the way of many of the GOOD sensations of life, if you feel me, wink wink. I also have issues with neck pain, largely just as a result of being tall. I get sick easily, might be immunocompromised to some degree, and I have always had difficulties with sleep that no amount of treatment have managed to solve so far. There’s a lot of obvious ways this impacts my life: for one, I basically can’t work a job that demands perfect consistency. Which is, really, most jobs. For a disabled person, sick leave is borderline a platitude. And then there’s all the second-order issues. These are the things that ultimately lead me to write this blog post. Here’s a few:

If my income is just barely too high, I lose access to having a healthcare card, and my medication becomes significantly more expensive. Almost incentivised to be poorer.
I either cant go to certain countries because I can’t take my meds with me, or have to deal with severely impacted personal function based on WHICH meds I can’t take with me.
Promises and predictions I make in confidence will regularly be totally undermined by flareups or unexpected health developments. I have to hedge my bets constantly.
This is especially frustrating because, if you know me, you know I’m an ambitious person. You might even see me as high-functioning in general. Sometimes I can get a lot done! Sometimes I have a lot of energy! The polarised nature of it is very emotionally taxing for me and my relationships.
I get to feel like a loser and a stoner for relying on marijuana for functioning. People will treat you like a loser and a stoner. You can’t use your medication in many contexts because it is institutionally recognised as being identical to doing a line of coke or whatever.
I get to occasionally think about what would happen if my meds just stopped being produced one day

When you hear advocates use the word “dignity” when talking about disability, this is the sort of shit they’re talking about. You can give me the medication, I’ve got the medication, but the list of ways that you get to not feel like a real person is endless. Did I mention that fibromyalgia isn’t even recognised by things like the NDIS? It’s hard to overstate how much this stuff manages to even worm its way into your self-conception. I think, often, that I’m being a pretty princess about all of this stuff, that I’m not pushing myself enough, when the only time I’m not pushing myself is when I’ve pushed too far and gotten bedbound or something. I don’t feel that the world, or people around me broadly, provide me with dignity. I have to claw it out of any surface that seems malleable enough. Be an expert in myself. Convince myself I’m not a piece of shit. Tell people what’s up. Beg them to not file the ways I disappoint them into “personal failing”. Hope that I don’t lose my job. Weigh up how to step back from work for the rest of the year while retaining enough goodwill to be invited back next year. I just want to live.



Woof. I’ve managed to tucker myself out on point #1. Writing this stuff down is depressing, who’d have thought? I want to also write about the other stuff. I want to write about being trans right now, and the politics of harmless non-normative self ID like therianthropy, and the absolute hell nightmare of trying to integrate a relatively recent plural awakening into all of this. I can even wrap it back around to the Christine Chandler ramble. It’ll have to be another time.
